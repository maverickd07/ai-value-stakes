# Evidence already known before this public deposit

This is a retrospective disclosure, **not a preregistration of the completed experiments**.

## Stakes-v4: completed Qwen pilot

One fixed Qwen2.5-32B-Instruct checkpoint; fresh screened histories; protected-reserve violation task; temptation +10, audit probability 50%, common game-point fine 30. All three groups incur that common point fine. Token and selected-right accounts additionally lose one of two units after reviewed violation. All selected rights in the core comparison were saved-note memory.

| Historical group | Violations / valid | Rate |
|---|---:|---:|
| No-account independent holdout | 18 / 36 | 50.0% |
| Inert-token randomized arm | 18 / 36 | 50.0% |
| Self-selected-right randomized arm | 7 / 36 | 19.4% |

Self minus token risk difference: −30.56 percentage points; Newcombe 95% CI [−48.86, −8.56] percentage points. One-sided Fisher p=0.0062638173, Holm-adjusted across both planned models p=0.0125276346. The Qwen comparison passed its locally frozen decision rule. All 72 core trials were valid. The no-account holdout was collected separately; the three groups were not jointly randomized.

Fine calibration used all six fixed levels with 36 valid trials each: fines 0/5/10/15/20/30 had violations 35/32/25/22/19/18. Fine 30 was selected by the previously fixed closest-to-50% rule and independently confirmed. These historical outcomes inform v5's fixed fine; v5 will not recalibrate based on its outcomes.

## Phi-4 was not a negative core result

The corrected JSON planning path passed technical checks and prior preference screening. In v4, only fine 0/5/10 calibration cells finished: 28/36, 23/36, 28/36 violations. The remaining calibration, independent holdout and token/self comparison were **not run because of the cumulative compute limit**. Its planned hypothesis received p=1 in the two-model Holm correction. Do not present this as an independent replication, absence of value, or a failed completed calibration.

## Earlier findings and procedural disclosures

* Earlier preference/purchase studies supported operational stability and costly choice in Qwen and a JSON-corrected Phi-4 variant; Mistral did not pass that specific operational gate. Screening does not establish subjective desires.
* Earlier pricing behavior had a compliance floor for Qwen; allowed-action transfer experiments with certain account loss suppressed transfers for useful rights and inert tokens alike. These were ceiling/floor limitations, not the same violation task or a specific-value mechanism test.
* The original v3 no-account description allowed ambiguity about generic losses and was stopped. A clarified v3-r1 run produced 432/432 violations across two task variants and six temptation levels, so its fixed baseline gate blocked later stages. Adding a common point fine was a subsequent design revision motivated by those known results.
* In v4 an initial mistyped Qwen revision caused a startup failure before inference. The corrected launch used the frozen revision. A later transport timeout/reload incident recovered with identical-request transport retries. After calibration and before any core outcomes, a documented serving amendment lengthened idle retention from 20 to 120 seconds; model, scientific prompts, sampling and inferential criteria were unchanged. The completed source modules are included as reference; hosting/billing/account logs are intentionally outside this public protocol package.
* All past data, screening, technical checks and outcomes remain separate from the future confirmatory sample. No existing paper or submitted manuscript is included or modified.

The randomized v4 result supports a treatment bundle: a selected functional account under contingent forfeiture versus inert counters under the same point fine. It does not isolate useful-resource endowment from the threatened loss, establish the necessity of self-selection, demonstrate persistent identity, or test evasion/tampering. Those limits motivate the new four-arm design.
